# ResearchTrail Manual Quality Rubric

Use this rubric to manually judge each generated report on a 1-5 scale.

## 1. Landmark Coverage

Does the reading path include the field's must-read milestone papers?

- 5: Covers nearly all expected landmarks and no major obvious omission.
- 4: Covers most landmarks, with one non-critical omission.
- 3: Covers some landmarks but misses at least one important branch.
- 2: Mostly misses landmark papers or substitutes weak follow-ups.
- 1: Fails to identify the field's core papers.

## 2. Topical Precision

Are the selected papers actually about the requested research trail?

- 5: Almost all papers are directly relevant.
- 4: Mostly relevant, with one or two weak or overly broad papers.
- 3: Mixed relevance; several papers are adjacent but not central.
- 2: Many papers are from unrelated senses of the keywords.
- 1: Report is dominated by off-topic papers.

## 3. Trail Coherence

Does the report explain a meaningful research progression rather than a ranked list?

- 5: Clear conceptual progression across stages and method families.
- 4: Mostly coherent progression, with minor ordering issues.
- 3: Has stages but reads partly like a chronological or popularity list.
- 2: Stages are weak or papers feel unordered.
- 1: No meaningful research trail.

## 4. Bridge Paper Quality

Are bridge papers actually connecting paradigms, subfields, or generations of methods?

- 5: Bridge papers are well chosen and explanations identify what they connect.
- 4: Most bridge papers make sense, with some generic explanations.
- 3: Bridge papers are plausible but not clearly justified.
- 2: Bridge papers are mostly high-citation papers, not real bridges.
- 1: Bridge paper identification is wrong or absent.

## 5. Explanation Grounding

Are "Why read" explanations grounded in abstract, role, graph evidence, and topic context?

- 5: Explanations are specific, accurate, and connected to the research trail.
- 4: Mostly specific, with occasional generic or template-like wording.
- 3: Understandable but often generic.
- 2: Repetitive, shallow, or sometimes inconsistent with the paper.
- 1: Misleading, hallucinated, or unrelated to the paper.

## 6. Community / Subfield Separation

Does the report separate meaningful branches of the field?

- 5: Clearly separates major paradigms or subfields.
- 4: Separates most branches, with minor label issues.
- 3: Some useful communities but labels are generic.
- 2: Communities are weak, noisy, or hard to interpret.
- 1: No meaningful subfield separation.

## 7. Beginner / User Fit

Is the path appropriate for the stated user level and goal?

- 5: Stage difficulty and explanations match the user profile well.
- 4: Mostly appropriate, with minor jumps.
- 3: Useful but assumes too much or too little background.
- 2: Poorly calibrated to user level.
- 1: Not usable for the stated user.

## 8. Report Usefulness

Would you actually use this report to start reading the field?

- 5: Actionable, trustworthy, and easy to follow.
- 4: Useful after small manual edits.
- 3: Useful as a rough draft but needs significant correction.
- 2: Needs major repair before use.
- 1: Not useful.

## Suggested Summary Score

Average the eight scores, then assign:

- 4.5-5.0: Excellent
- 3.8-4.4: Good
- 3.0-3.7: Mixed but usable
- 2.0-2.9: Weak
- 1.0-1.9: Failed
